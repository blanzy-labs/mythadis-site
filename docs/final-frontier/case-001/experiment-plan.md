## 2. Falsifiable hypothesis and registration gate

Conditional operational hypothesis: with initial capital 68 units, no further deposits, the source-specified system and rules produce an ending net realizable value of at least 6,732 units (balance interpretation), or 6,800 units (profit interpretation), using the interpretation approved from the exact source by the exact source-defined deadline. Lock market, period/time zone, model/version, instructions, prices, fees, spread, slippage, leverage and failure handling BEFORE examining outcomes. If those details cannot be obtained, the claim remains insufficiently specified; do not substitute a generic chatbot or investment strategy.

This paper test concerns arithmetic/replay feasibility, not demonstrated AI causality or actual withdrawability. A statement that this *can* happen is not falsified by a few failed runs. A universal guarantee can be challenged by a valid in-scope counterexample, but only with all stated conditions preserved. Typical-return claims need representative sampling and an agreed success frequency, not an invented threshold.

## 3. Supporting evidence

Preserved original claim with context; reproducible, timestamped inputs and instructions; complete trade/account ledger including failures and losses; consistent fee/financing accounting; independently checkable price records. A paper replay meeting the threshold supports only simulated feasibility under declared assumptions. Real performance/withdrawability would need independently verifiable existing records, not a dashboard screenshot. Successful outcomes alone do not isolate the contribution of AI.

## 4. Undermining evidence

A valid failure contradicts an unconditional universal guarantee within its defined scope. Missing funds, added deposits, omitted losses/costs, changed deadlines or selected winners undermine an advertised example's interpretation. Missing documentation establishes an evidence gap, not fraud or impossibility. A paper run that misses the threshold establishes only failure for that simulation and assumptions.

## Required-performance worksheet — mathematical analysis, not trading results

For equal fully reinvested net returns per round, `ending = 68 × (1 + r_net)^n`. Therefore `r_net = (target / 68)^(1/n) − 1`.

| Net completed rounds (illustrative, not source-derived) | $6,732 ending balance (99×) | $6,732 profit / $6,800 balance (100×) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 9,800% | 9,900% |
| 10 | 58.330% each | 58.489% each |
| 100 | 4.702% each | 4.713% each |

These are required equal net gains, not predicted trades or evidence of impossibility. Assumptions: all capital reused, no losses, deposits or withdrawals, perfect compounding, no capital locked across concurrent trades. Monitoring 50 markets does not multiply available capital by 50. Without a defined overnight interval, do not infer a trades-per-hour rate.

For a simple unleveraged sequential buy/sell model, if the pre-cost sale-value multiplier is `1 + g` and proportional buy/sell fees are `f_b`, `f_s`, the post-fee round multiplier is `(1 + g) × (1 − f_s) / (1 + f_b)`. Hence the required gross gain is `(1 + r_net) × (1 + f_b) / (1 − f_s) − 1`. This accounting model is illustrative; exchange-specific charging conventions must replace it when known. Fixed commissions, spread, slippage, financing, transfer/withdrawal fees and failed executions require ledger entries; do not invent Binance fee rates. Taxes and software costs should be disclosed separately from trading P&L, with their treatment defined before analysis.

Leverage can magnify gains and losses; financing, liquidation and margin constraints must be included. Quoted arbitrage gaps are not guaranteed executable fills: both legs, latency, order-book depth, minimum order size, partial fills and available capital matter. Do not infer AI causality from code-generation assistance. Authentic trade logs, exchange/account statements and reconciliation with balances are necessary to assess an advertised historical result; a screenshot alone or a new bot replay cannot verify what the unnamed trader actually did.
