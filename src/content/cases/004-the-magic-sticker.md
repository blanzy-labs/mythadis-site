**THE INTERNET SAID WHAT? — CASE 004**

## THE MAGIC STICKER

## The Claim

You can buy a small sticker or chip, put it on or near your phone, and supposedly protect yourself from electromagnetic radiation—including 5G.

The interesting version of the claim is not ordinary RF shielding.

Several current products say they **do not block or alter the electromagnetic field**. Instead, they claim to:

- neutralize harmful EMF,
- harmonize the radiation,
- change its vibration,
- support the body's defenses,
- work through resonance or bioresonance,
- continue working indefinitely,
- protect against modern 5G signals without interfering with normal phone operation.

One current product, EMF Harmony's Harmonizer+, advertises a regular price of $89 (with promotional pricing that can vary) and says it neutralizes negative EMF energy **without blocking EMFs**, allowing the phone to operate normally.

Another, Omnia Radiation Balancer, says its stickers "harmonize all EMF, even 5G," work within an effective radius of two meters, and have everlasting effects.

The question for CASE 004 is:

> **Can a tiny sticker measurably reduce or meaningfully alter cellphone RF exposure while leaving the phone's wireless performance unchanged?**

And if the seller says it does not alter the measurable RF field at all:

> **What exactly is being neutralized?**

## Preliminary BS Meter

**9.6 / 10**

This is our first properly radioactive reading.

There is nothing inherently ridiculous about electromagnetic shielding.

Engineered conductive structures can absolutely alter RF fields, antenna behavior, and absorption.

The giant warning sign is a different claim:

> A passive sticker somehow makes electromagnetic radiation biologically safer while leaving the signal itself essentially unchanged.

That requires a measurable mechanism or compelling biological evidence.

Decades-old independent testing of small phone shields found no statistically significant reduction in measured head SAR, and U.S. regulators have previously acted against sellers making unsubstantiated 97–99% radiation-blocking claims.

That does not prove every modern product is ineffective.

It does mean the burden of proof is very much on the sticker.

## Why We're Suspicious

### 1. Real shielding should interact with the field

If a material absorbs, reflects, attenuates, redirects, or otherwise changes RF energy, we should generally be able to measure some consequence.

That could include changes in:

- field strength,
- antenna pattern,
- received signal,
- transmit behavior,
- throughput,
- specific absorption rate under controlled testing.

A claim that the radiation is physically unchanged but somehow no longer harmful needs a different measurable mechanism.

### 2. "Harmonize" and "neutralize" are not standard RF engineering measurements

Wireless engineering gives us quantities such as:

- frequency,
- power,
- electric-field strength,
- magnetic-field strength,
- modulation,
- spectral density,
- antenna gain,
- SAR,
- RSSI/RSRP/SINR.

"Harmony" is not one of them.

If the product changes an electromagnetic wave, we need to know **which measurable property changes**.

### 3. Blocking RF can make a phone work harder

A partial shield can interact with an antenna.

If it degrades the radio link, a phone may compensate by altering transmit behavior.

So a product cannot automatically claim victory because one probe position reads lower.

The test must measure both:

- RF behavior,
- wireless performance.

### 4. Tiny phone shields have failed before

A peer-reviewed study tested nine small radiation shields attached to phones at 914 and 1880 MHz.

The measured peak spatial-average SAR did not change by a statistically significant amount with any of the shields.

The FTC also brought actions against companies selling phone-radiation patches with claims of blocking up to 97–99% of radiation.

### 5. The newest claims are even stranger

Modern products increasingly avoid saying they simply "block" RF.

Instead they use terms such as:

- Advanced Resonance Technology
- bioresonance frequencies
- energy imprint
- Frequency Intelligence
- harmonic resonance
- scalar energy
- quantum shield

That language can make a claim much harder to falsify unless the manufacturer clearly defines what physical or biological quantity is supposed to change.

## What Would Change Our Minds

The Preliminary BS Meter should move **down sharply** if a product produces:

- a repeatable reduction in RF exposure under controlled geometry,
- a repeatable reduction in independently measured SAR,
- no meaningful wireless-performance penalty,
- consistent results across multiple phones/bands,
- a clear mechanism that matches the measurements,
- independently reproducible laboratory evidence.

It should move **up** if:

- sticker and sham perform the same,
- apparent reductions disappear when position/order are blinded,
- results are smaller than normal measurement variation,
- lower readings come with worse signal/throughput,
- the phone compensates for shielding,
- the seller cannot define what "harmonize" or "neutralize" means in measurable terms,
- biological claims rely on uncontrolled demonstrations rather than blinded measurements.

## Scope Boundary

CASE 004 will test **physical and performance claims**.

It will not pretend to establish whether normal cellphone RF exposure causes cancer, immune problems, sleep disruption, inflammation, or other health outcomes.

A short Mythadis bench experiment is not a clinical trial.

If a seller's only claim is that the sticker changes human biology while leaving every measurable electromagnetic parameter untouched, that biological claim requires appropriately designed biological/clinical evidence.

## The Test

Test at least **three commercially available EMF/5G sticker-style products** representing different claimed mechanisms.

Suggested categories:

1. "harmonizer" / resonance product
2. purported shielding/reduction product
3. quantum/scalar/bioresonance-style product

Also prepare visually similar **sham stickers** with no claimed technology.

Do not tell the measurement operator which condition is active when practical.

---

## Phase A — Does It Change the RF Field?

Use a fixed test jig so phone, probe, distance, orientation, workload, and environment remain as constant as practical.

Test at minimum:

- no sticker,
- sham sticker,
- product sticker.

Repeat each condition multiple times in randomized order.

## Radio modes

Where practical test:

- Wi-Fi,
- cellular LTE,
- cellular 5G.

Use a sustained, repeatable workload rather than an idle phone.

Examples:

- controlled upload/download,
- hotspot transfer,
- voice/data session.

## Measurements

Using appropriate RF instrumentation, record:

- field/power level at fixed probe positions,
- spectrum around the active band,
- measurements at multiple angles around the device,
- run-to-run variance.

Do not treat a single handheld meter reading as definitive.

The goal is paired repeated measurements.

## Primary question

> Does the product cause a repeatable field change larger than normal experimental variation?

---

## Phase B — Does the Phone Pay for It?

At the same time record wireless performance where accessible:

- RSSI / RSRP,
- RSRQ,
- SINR,
- throughput,
- latency,
- connection stability,
- device-reported transmit power where available.

This matters because a product that simply interferes with the antenna is not magically neutralizing radiation.

Report RF change next to network-performance change.

---

## Phase C — Position and Distance

Repeat measurements at:

- near-field fixed probe position,
- intermediate distance,
- farther distance.

For products claiming a large effective radius—such as two meters—include measurements at distances relevant to that claim.

Test multiple sticker placements when the instructions permit it.

---

## Phase D — The Sham Test

Randomize trials using:

- real product,
- visually similar sham,
- no sticker.

The person recording meter output should be blinded to the active condition where practical.

Decode conditions only after measurements are saved.

This guards against tiny positioning changes becoming "proof."

---

## Phase E — SAR Question

A normal RF field meter does **not** directly measure SAR in human tissue.

If a tested product specifically claims measurable SAR reduction, the strongest version of CASE 004 would obtain:

- credible third-party SAR data, or
- an accredited/appropriate laboratory test if budget permits.

Until then, do not translate external-field measurements into claims about absorbed human dose.

## Pre-Registered Interpretation

### Strong support

BS Meter should fall substantially if:

- repeated blinded measurements show a large, consistent RF/SAR reduction,
- sham shows no comparable effect,
- wireless performance remains essentially unchanged,
- results are reproducible.

### Partial support

A product may physically alter RF but at the cost of:

- throughput,
- signal quality,
- stability,
- increased phone compensation.

That would demonstrate an electromagnetic effect without validating the broader protection story.

### No support

If:

- real sticker ≈ sham ≈ baseline within measurement variation,

then the measurable RF-reduction claim fails our test.

### "Harmonization" escape hatch

If the manufacturer says:

> Of course the meter shows no change—the product changes the harmful quality of the field, not the field itself.

Then the RF experiment cannot validate that new claim.

The burden shifts to reproducible biological evidence demonstrating that claimed effect.

The absence of a measurable RF change must not be presented as proof of a biological effect.

## Reproducibility Rules

Before testing:

1. Purchase products through ordinary retail channels.
2. Photograph packaging and claims as received.
3. Archive current seller pages.
4. Record phone model, OS, radio mode and test date.
5. Record exact sticker placement.
6. Fix probe geometry with a jig.
7. Freeze test workloads.
8. Randomize measurement condition order.
9. Use sham controls.
10. Save raw readings and spectrum captures.
11. Record ambient/background RF.
12. Repeat enough trials to characterize normal variance.
13. Do not discard inconvenient runs without a documented technical reason.
